Part two

You are not the only one guessing

This is not only a high school problem. A survey of college students found that most default to rereading their notes and textbooks when studying alone, and few spontaneously test themselves on the material, even though retrieval practice is one of the most effective ways to actually learn something.[1]

A wider review of study techniques reached a similar conclusion: the two strategies students reach for most, rereading and highlighting, are among the least effective for long-term learning, while techniques like practice testing and spaced practice work far better and are rarely taught.[2]

  1. [1] Karpicke, J. D., Butler, A. C., and Roediger, H. L. "Metacognitive strategies in student learning: do students practise retrieval when they study on their own?" Memory, 2009.
  2. [2] Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., and Willingham, D. T. "Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques: promising directions from cognitive and educational psychology." Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 2013.
